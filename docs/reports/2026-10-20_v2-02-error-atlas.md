## 1. What arrived

## 2. What it is in v2

The key is the **sealed set** (prod-chunks rule 3): the one population that is never used for selection, calibration, fitting, mining or threshold choice, read once per phase close. In code:

- `hearsay.benchmark.load_sealed_population()` loads it as population `test_key`, role `sealed`, joined to the test manifest by filename with exact coverage both ways. It is deliberately **not** part of `load_populations()`, so no `Bench`, scorecard, rule or selection path ever holds the labels. `require_selectable` refuses the role.
- `read_sealed()` is the only reader. It goes through the sealed set's own ledger (`outputs/v2/benchmark/sealed_ledger/` authority, `benchmarks/sealed_ledger.jsonl` tracked copy, same mechanics as the holdout ledger: O_EXCL authority file, per-key lock, audit JSONL), keyed by the rule definition and the labels' sha. It has **no reference exemption**, and every read must name the phase it closes and a reason of at least 20 characters, both persisted in the ledger entry.
- CLI: `uv run python scripts/v2_benchmark.py sealed (--tsv SUB.tsv | --file X.csv --name N) --phase P --reason "..."`. Readout: the three readings + EER, FA/miss counts at each reading's min-cost threshold and at the decision threshold, a per-clip stratified bootstrap, and the per-file error list (every miss and false alarm, plus the 50 highest-scoring reals and 50 lowest-scoring spoofs), so that the atlas never has to open the key again.
- Registry `benchmarks/v1.yaml` bumped to **v1.2**: `populations.test_key`, the key file in `inputs`, `deferred.sealed_set` moved to `resolved`.
- Tests: `tests/test_benchmark.py` (sealed section, 9 tests; the hermetic world now carries a 10-row key; the autouse fixture isolates the sealed ledger and guards its tracked copy).

## 3. The Phase 0 read: v1's shipped TSV on the key

One ledgered read (`sealed_ledger.jsonl` key `0ea8c4e0b353…`, phase "Phase 0", lane v2-01): `submissions/CrossExam_predictions.tsv` (sha256 `fb783076…`). Readout `outputs/v2/benchmark/sealed/SHIP_tsv.json`.

| What | Population | brief | package-0.5 | package-0.3 | EER |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SHIP_tsv | test_key (sealed) | 0.0537 | 0.1543 | **0.0733** | 0.0362 |

- **NSA's returned draft-review number (minDCF 0.0733, EER 3.534%) is reproduced exactly by the package reading at Pspoof 0.3** (`min_cost(y, s, 0.3, c_fa=1, c_miss=4)` = P_FA + 1.714·P_miss). Not by the brief's reading (0.0537) and not by package-0.5 (0.1543), which rule 5 currently names as the primary selection reading. Our `eer()` gives 3.62% against NSA's 3.53%; the crossing point itself is at 3.53% / 3.54% (P_FA / P_miss), so the gap is the interpolation convention, not the data.
- Stratified per-clip bootstrap (2,000 draws), package-0.3: p5 0.0466, p50 0.0704, p95 0.0962.
- FA / miss counts: at 0.5 (the shipped decision threshold) **1 false alarm / 1,191 real (0.084%) and 24 misses / 480 spoof (5.0%)**. At the metric's own optimum for package-0.3 (threshold 0.400): 15 FA / 17 misses. At the brief's optimum (0.466): 1 FA / 22 misses. The whole cost is misses; v1's selection pressure against false alarms shows.
- **Decided (Nathan, Oct 20, after this read): package-0.3 is the primary reading** (rule 5). The earlier verdicts (V2-60, V2-33 stage 0, V2-53's readouts) were gated on package-0.5; their restatement is `docs/reports/2026-10-20_v2-verdicts-under-package03.md`.

## 4. The atlas of the 25 errors

`scripts/v2_error_atlas.py` joins the readout's error list with the unlabelled per-file material the v1 runner produced for every test clip (`outputs/runner/v2_full/`: the sidecar's fused inputs and the per-file JSON's detector features and routing log), the durations manifest, and the WavLM Large probe's test logits (`outputs/detector_scores/wavlm_l.csv`, a column that was built and never shipped). Output `outputs/v2/error_atlas.csv` (100 rows: 25 errors + the worst-50 lists). The key is never opened by the script.

### 4.1 Where the misses sit

Test filenames are anonymised (`HGT*.wav`), and the key carries no generator or speaker, so **generator identity is not recoverable** for any miss.

| feature (median) | misses (n=24) | all test (n=1,671) | miss p25–p75 |
|---|---|---|---|
| duration (s) | 3.60 | 3.41 | 3.22–4.09 |
| effective bandwidth (Hz) | 7,500 | 7,250 | 7,250–7,500 |
| 7–8 kHz band level (dB) | −44.5 | −48.2 | −48.5 to −41.3 |
| voiced fraction | **0.535** | 0.707 | 0.45–0.74 |
| RMS (dBFS) | −18.6 | −18.1 | −19.6 to −16.8 |
| speaker-drift min cosine | **0.162** | 0.273 | 0.03–0.29 |
| m1b_v3 logit | −1.50 | −3.46 | −3.97 to −0.84 |
| m5_xlsr_ft logit | −1.50 | −2.31 | −2.28 to −0.52 |
| spectra_aasist logit | **+6.33** | −5.22 | +1.6 to +8.4 |
| wavlm_l logit | **+0.87** | −1.84 | +0.28 to +1.88 |

- **Not a short-clip story.** 9 of 24 misses are under 3.5 s (38%) against 56% of the test set.
- **Not a gate or bandwidth story.** The speech gate passed all 24; 9 of 24 sit at or below the 7.25 kHz band match, against 59% of the test set, so the misses are if anything the wider-band clips.
- **Not an E-suppression story.** The Spectra suppression step fired on 1 of the 24 (`HGT5597873`, the one miss where Spectra also says real); 47 test clips in all.
- **What they share:** less voiced speech (median voiced fraction 0.54 vs 0.71: more pause, breath or non-speech frames) and more within-clip voice variation (speaker-drift min cosine 0.16 vs 0.27). Both are consistent with longer-form, expressive or multi-sentence synthesis rather than the clean single-sentence DiffSSD style v1 trained on. A hypothesis to test, not a result.

### 4.2 Which column would have caught each miss

A column "ranks a clip synthetic" here if that clip's percentile within the test set, under that column alone, is above 0.713 (one minus the key's spoof share), i.e. the column puts it in its top 28.7%. This uses only unlabelled ranks plus the 24 identities from the readout.

| column | misses ranked synthetic | shipped weight |
|---|---|---|
| m1b_v3 (the carrier) | **3 / 24** | 0.6 |
| handcrafted_v5 | 4 / 24 | 0.2 |
| m5_xlsr_ft | 6 / 24 | 0.2 |
| spectra_aasist (suppressor only, never promotes) | **18 / 24** | 0 |
| wavlm_l (built, not shipped) | **16 / 24** | 0 |
| any column | 23 / 24 | |
| none | 1 / 24 (`HGT1446333`) | |

The per-file table is in the script's output and `outputs/v2/error_atlas.csv`. The pattern is the one v1's retrospective suspected (B.4, "Letting Spectra promote files would recover 76 of the 158 ITW misses"): the shipped rule gave 60% of the weight to the column that is blind to these clips, and 0% to the two columns that see them. v1 made that choice on purpose (Spectra's training data is undisclosed; WavLM failed the pre-declared gate on inner CV). The key does not overturn the reasoning, it measures its price: on this test set, the misses that cost 0.0733 are mostly clips Spectra and WavLM already rank as synthetic.

**What this does not say.** It does not say Spectra or WavLM would score better than the shipped rule on the key; that is a different sealed read per column, and taking five of them in Phase 0 is Nathan's call (one ledger entry "v1 column reference rows" would be the disciplined form). It does not say promoting on Spectra is safe: 29.3% of the test set has Spectra above 0 and 27.3% has the shipped score above 0.5, so a promotion rule would also move reals.

### 4.3 The one false alarm

`HGT7056492.wav` (3.46 s, bona fide, shipped 0.676). Only m1b_v3 leans synthetic (+0.43, 73rd percentile); handcrafted −0.68, M5 −0.99, WavLM −1.06 and Spectra −2.22 all say real. Spectra's margin sits just above the −3.0 suppression cutoff, so E did not fire; a cutoff at −2 would have caught it and, on the test set, moved other clips too. Wide band (7,500 Hz), clean voice (drift min cosine 0.48, voiced 79%). No recording-chain feature marks it; it is a carrier error with no corroboration, which is the case the orchestrator was meant to catch and did not, because the only veto it has is Spectra's below −3.

The next eleven highest-scoring reals (0.418–0.453) are all below 0.5 and all have Spectra at −3.6 to −6.9: the suppression margin is what kept v1's false-alarm count at one.
