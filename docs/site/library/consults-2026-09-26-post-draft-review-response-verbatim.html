Executive summary

Scope

Post-draft triage for the NSA HEARSAY audio-deepfake challenge, ~13 hours before the Sunday 05:00 EDT freeze. Interprets the single NSA readout on the shipped TSV (minDCF 0.0733, EER 3.534%) — regime, error profile, and evidentiary weight — then ranks the four candidate change classes (SSL heads, engineered features, fusion redesign, or stop touching the file) under the team's fold-discipline, regression-tolerance, and single-ratifier constraints, and allocates the remaining clock.

Conclusion

The (minDCF, EER) pair cannot identify NSA's cost convention, but the EER tightly constrains each conditional profile: under the brief cost the optimum is forced to $\le$~5 false alarms plus 18–37 misses; under the sponsor cost it inverts to ~42–86 false alarms plus $\le$5 misses. NSA's verbal statement of the brief cost — not the numbers — makes the few-FA/miss-tail read the working hypothesis. One aggregate readout cannot validate any fusion change; all fusion redesign should stop. Keep the shipped TSV as presumptive final and run only 3–4 bounded, pre-declared experiments (WavLM Large probe first, XLS-R layer 5–9 probe second, a second M3 suppression tier as a free audit, noise-twin handcrafted in a parallel session) under a gate stricter than the standing rule. If nothing clears by ~18:30–19:00 EDT, freeze and sleep.

Confidence: Medium

N_S=500
m=0.0733
eer=0.03534
# Per error increments
brief_fa=9.33/N_R
brief_miss=1/N_S
sponsor_fa=1/N_R
sponsor_miss=4/N_S
print('per error', brief_fa, brief_miss, sponsor_fa, sponsor_miss)
print('equiv current brief', m/brief_fa, m/brief_miss)
print('equiv current sponsor', m/sponsor_fa, m/sponsor_miss)
gap=m-0.03
print('gap equivalents brief', gap/brief_fa, gap/brief_miss)
print('gap equivalents sponsor', gap/sponsor_fa, gap/sponsor_miss)
print('EER counts', eer*N_R, eer*N_S)
print('cost at EER brief/sponsor', (9.33+1)*eer, (1+4)*eer)
# Conditional fixed-threshold standard error over continuous allocations satisfying total cost.
# brief: 9.33 pfa + pmiss = m
pfa_b=np.linspace(0,m/9.33,100001)
pm_b=m-9.33*pfa_b
se_b=np.sqrt((9.33**2)*pfa_b*(1-pfa_b)/N_R + pm_b*(1-pm_b)/N_S)
print('brief p ranges', pfa_b[0],pfa_b[-1],pm_b.min(),pm_b.max())
print('brief SE range/max loc',se_b.min(),se_b.max(),pfa_b[se_b.argmax()],pm_b[se_b.argmax()], '95 max',1.96*se_b.max())
# sponsor: pfa + 4 pm = m
pfa_s=np.linspace(0,m,100001)
pm_s=(m-pfa_s)/4
se_s=np.sqrt(pfa_s*(1-pfa_s)/N_R + (4**2)*pm_s*(1-pm_s)/N_S)
print('sponsor p ranges', pfa_s.min(),pfa_s.max(),pm_s.min(),pm_s.max())
print('sponsor SE range/max loc',se_s.min(),se_s.max(),pfa_s[se_s.argmax()],pm_s[se_s.argmax()], '95 max',1.96*se_s.max())
# integer pairs within nearest output rounding 0.00005 around .0733
pairs_b=[]

        c=9.33*fa/N_R+miss/N_S

            pairs_b.append((fa,miss,c))
print('brief integer pairs within .00005, first/last/count:',pairs_b[:10],pairs_b[-10:],len(pairs_b))
pairs_s=[]

            pairs_s.append((fa,miss,c))

1. What the returned pair does and does not identify

The low returned values establish only that NSA evaluated the file with an effective polarity compatible with its ranking; they do not establish whether NSA read 1.0 as synthetic directly or inverted into the official ASVspoof 5 convention, where higher scores represent bona fide speech [S3]. "Direction is ours" is therefore slightly too strong as stated, though operationally moot: the pre-declared 0.00–0.20 branch is correctly triggered, and no flip or fallback applies. Calling the regime "holdout-like" is wrong — at roughly 11× the holdout $\mathrm{minDCF}$ and ~17× its $\mathrm{EER}$, the test set is a distinct, moderately shifted domain whose cost-relevant low-FA tail held up far better than In-the-Wild's.

On the cost convention: at the equal-error point the brief cost would be $(9.33+1)\times 0.03534 = 0.365$ and the sponsor cost $5\times 0.03534 = 0.177$; a minimum of 0.0733 is feasible under either curve, so the pair alone is non-identifying. But the EER constrains each conditional decomposition sharply (this is the synthesis of Gemini's feasibility argument with ChatGPT's arithmetic):

  • Brief cost $(9.33P_{FA}+P_{miss}=0.0733)$: $P_{FA}\ge\mathrm{EER}$ would cost $\ge 0.33$ alone, so the optimum forces $P_{miss}\in[3.53\%, 7.33\%]$ (≈18–37 missed fakes) and $P_{FA}\le 0.41\%$ (≈0–5 false alarms).
  • Sponsor cost $(P_{FA}+4P_{miss}=0.0733)$: $P_{miss}\ge\mathrm{EER}$ would cost $\ge 0.14$ alone, so the optimum forces $P_{FA}\in[3.53\%, 7.33\%]$ (≈42–86 false alarms) and $P_{miss}\le 0.95\%$ (≈0–5 misses).

So the profiles are mutually exclusive and each is essentially forced once the convention is fixed — the convention itself is resolved by NSA's in-person statement of the analyst scenario (false alarm 4× a miss), not by the readout. Working read, stated with explicit confidence: few-FA/miss-tail, conditional on the brief convention; the conditioning assumption is briefing-derived, roughly 80–85% not 100%. The further attribution of those misses to unseen generators is plausible mechanism (the M3 MLAAD probe evidence) but is inference, not measurement — and note that M3, restricted to suppression-only, can only move files toward real and therefore cannot repair a miss tail regardless of how good it is at recognizing unseen spoofs. Two related corrections from ChatGPT stand: M5 is not a short-clip expert (holdout 0.60 on clips $\le 4$ s), and since $\mathrm{minDCF}$ is an oracle-threshold ranking metric [S3], the Platt map and any order-preserving recalibration cannot move it — spend zero clock on calibration.
